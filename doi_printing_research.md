# DoI Printing Research: Finding the 4-Page Octavo Edition

## Objective

Identify the specific historical printing of the Declaration of Independence that Ward used as the physical key text. The phase 8 hoax construction model predicts a **4-page octavo printing with ~325 words per page**.

## What We Know

**The Beale DoI**: 1311 words. Uses "inalienable" (Jefferson's draft form, not the official "unalienable"). Has 8 specific textual modifications from the standard DoI. The model predicts a 4-page printing with ~325 words/page.

**The Ward pamphlet itself**: 23 pages total, DoI starts on p.17, with numbered words. The numbering adds bulk so the DoI likely spans 5 pages in the pamphlet — but this is the *keyed* version, not the printing the hoaxer would have worked from.

## The Math Check

A standard 19th-century octavo page (~6×9") with period-typical typesetting yields roughly 250-350 words per page. At 325 wpp:
- 4 pages = 1300 words
- Beale DoI = 1311 words
- Error: 11 words (~0.8%) — easily within rounding of a real page break

This is very consistent. The DoI fills 4 octavo pages almost exactly.

## Most Promising Candidates

### 1. "The American's Guide" (Hogan & Thompson, Philadelphia, 1838/1840)

Octavo compilation of DoI + Articles of Confederation + Constitution + state constitutions. Digitized at LOC. The DoI would be the opening section and likely spans ~4 pages. Widely available, octavo format, right era. **Strongest specific candidate found.**

- LOC: https://www.loc.gov/item/17004893/
- Digitized images: https://www.loc.gov/resource/gdcmassbookdig.americansguideco00unse/

### 2. July 4th Oration Pamphlets

Very common in the 1820s-1850s. Typically printed the DoI text alongside the oration. Many were octavo or duodecimo. Strong class of candidates but no specific one identified.

### 3. School Readers / Textbooks

The DoI appeared in many 19th-century textbooks. Format would be right. Not yet investigated.

### 4. Townsends Reproduction (modern, based on period source)

4 pages exactly at 7.75" × 4.75" (small octavo/large duodecimo). Modern reproduction but confirms the full DoI fits on exactly 4 pages in small octavo format. Uses "leader words" (catchwords) at page bottoms — a practice phased out by ~1810.

- https://www.townsends.us/products/declaration-of-the-united-states

### 5. Standalone Pamphlet Printings

Poorly documented online. These would be the most direct match but are the hardest to find in digital catalogues.

## Eliminated Formats

| Format | Why Not |
|--------|---------|
| Dunlap broadside (1776) | Single sheet, not paginated |
| Stone engraving (1823) | Broadside ~26×29", single sheet |
| Force reprints (1833) | Broadside format |
| Newspaper reprints | Column format, not paginated as standalone |

## The "Inalienable" Constraint

The Beale DoI uses **"inalienable"** (Jefferson's draft form), not **"unalienable"** (official engrossed copy / Stone engraving). Any candidate printing must also use "inalienable." This narrows the field significantly:

- Jefferson's original drafts: "inalienable"
- Official engrossed parchment: "unalienable"
- Dunlap broadside: "unalienable"
- Stone engraving (1823) and derivatives: "unalienable"
- Most post-1823 reprints: "unalienable"

Printings that used "inalienable" likely derived from Jefferson's draft tradition rather than the official engrossed copy. This is a strong discriminator.

## Recommended Next Steps

1. **Access the LOC digitized "American's Guide"** (1838/1840) and count which pages the DoI occupies — most likely match among what's findable online
2. **Check Viemeister's book** — he reproduced the original 1885 pamphlet in Chapter 3 (pp.22-44) and may have identified the source DoI printing
3. **Early American Imprints database** (Shaw-Shoemaker) — catalogues standalone DoI pamphlets from 1800-1860 with physical descriptions including page counts
4. **Search for "inalienable" printings** — cross-reference the inalienable/unalienable split with known printings to narrow candidates
5. **Virginia printing history** — check Library of Virginia, UVA Special Collections for DoI printings available in Lynchburg/Bedford area
